What We Added to homr

Structured notation heads, a page-level text stage, decode-time cross-staff reranking, and a scan-adapted checkpoint — plus the traps we hit getting there. Written for people who work on the codebase.

Engineering write-up · every figure from a held-out split

TL;DR

  • Nothing breaks. Every addition is off by default; several are zero-gated so enabling them is still a no-op until trained. Your existing checkpoints load and behave identically.
  • homr can represent beams now — full six-level state, 95.1% exact-vector on held-out. Also ties, slur spans/sides, stem direction.
  • Recognition improved on real scans — every branch, +2.1 to +3.5 pts on vocal, +1.8 to +6.3 on instrumental, vs. the pinned upstream checkpoint.
  • Decode-time reranking cuts structural findings 20.8% over 200 pages, zero pages made worse.
  • New Stage 3: page-level text detection (lyrics/dynamics/tempo/staff-text/expression).
  • Four things didn't work. They're written up too, so you don't spend the week we did.

First question: will this break my checkpoint?

No, and that was a hard design constraint rather than a hope. The rule we held to:

a checkpoint trained without a feature must keep loading and behaving exactly as before

In configs.py that looks like this — every extension defaults off:

# Structured notation heads (beams, stem direction, slurs). Off by default: they
# are output-only additions, and a checkpoint trained without them must keep
# loading and behaving exactly as before.
self.enable_structured_heads = False
self.enable_profile_context  = False
self.duration_adherence_weight    = 0.0
self.cross_staff_coherence_weight = 0.0

Two of those go further than a flag. profile_context and the cross-staff adapter have zero-initialised gates, so even switching them on is a no-op until training moves the gate. That means you can land the code, ship it, and decide later whether to train it — which is the property that makes this contributable rather than a fork.

Checkpoint loading validates an allowlist of expected new parameters and rejects everything else. An absent core weight is an error, not a silent reinitialisation — which matters more than it sounds, because a silently reinitialised encoder produces plausible-looking garbage.

Design rule worth stealing

New semantics become new heads, never an expanded rhythm vocabulary. Growing that vocabulary would alter the biggest existing softmax and embedding matrices and entangle notation fidelity with note-sequence accuracy. Keep the thing that already works untouched.

Structured notation heads

Eleven heads read the decoder's hidden state and predict, per note: six beam levels, stem direction, tie state, dynamic mark, and per-slot slur event and side. Head names are stable identifiers (beam.level.1, stem.direction, slur.slot.1.event…) and the trained manifest declares which ones a checkpoint actually supports — a name absent from the manifest means unsupported, not "confidently predicts nothing."

Training runs with the core frozen. From a real run:

checkpoint: loaded 326 parameters; initialized 22 new ones
            under decoder.structured_heads
training 22 tensor(s), everything else frozen

Freezing isn't an optimisation, it's what makes the result mean something. If the core moved too, a gain could be the heads or the core drifting to suit them.

Don't trust two of those rows

Beam level 4 has 8 supported positions in the entire split — that 0.5972 is arithmetic, not a measurement. Dynamics at 0.103 is a rarity artifact: 94.5% of notes carry none, and the labelled rest spreads over 11 classes, five with <13 examples. Macro-F1 weights classes equally, so a head that nails p/f/sf and can't learn fz still scores ~0.10.

The generator couldn't write beams

Worth its own section because it's the kind of thing that hides for months. The heads predicted beams at 95% and music_xml_generator.py contained zero beam references. Every prediction was thrown away on the way out, and renders showed the engraver's automatic beaming — which we'd already measured as rewriting the grouping of 1.7% of notes, its biggest single pattern turning backward hooks into full beams.

build_beams() now emits per-level elements. Four edges, each of which fails silently:

  • Hooks need MusicXML's spelling — forward hook, not our internal forward_hook. Wrong one → the engraver ignores it.
  • flag and not_applicable emit nothing. Absence is how MusicXML says "not beamed"; writing anything asserts a connection that isn't there.
  • Inapplicable levels must not renumber later ones, or a 16th beam gets written as an 8th.
  • Notes with no structured labels emit exactly what they did before — so checkpoints without the heads are untouched.
The bit that stung

Nine unit tests passed while the real path emitted zero beams. The end-to-end check caught it: my probe called attach_sidecar(symbols, path) with the args reversed. Green unit tests are not evidence a feature works.

Decode-time cross-staff reranking

This is the biggest end-to-end quality win and it needs no retraining at all.

Ensemble scores hand you a constraint a single-staff decoder can't use: sibling staves in a system must agree about where barlines fall. So at each staff's narrowest-margin rhythm decisions, fork a full alternate decode via the existing generate_from_prefix, then keep whichever candidate best matches the other staves' majority cumulative barline positions.

Deliberately narrower than real beam search — that would need per-step KV-cache batching, and decoder_inference.py is batch=1 throughout. Much bigger build, same stated goal.

It sits on top of deterministic cross-staff analysis — eight consistency checks running live, firing on 71.4% of pages, with repair proposals on 14.6%. The ordering was deliberate: checks, then targeted repair, then a learned adapter only if the cheap things fall short. The adapter is still unbuilt and zero-gated, and that's a success condition, not a TODO.

Two things before you quote these. The absolute levels differ between domains because the tasks differ — OSSQ staves are single quartet lines, Lieder staves are dense piano-and-voice. Compare within a column pair, not across. And this metric is stricter than teacher-forced eval_accuracy: both sides are padded to the longer length with a sentinel that can't match, so a length disagreement counts against you rather than being scored on the overlap only.

Also: it's a combined delta. Corrected pagination, carried clefs, restored barlines and scan adaptation all landed in one checkpoint. It says the programme worked; it doesn't tell you which change earned what.

Stage 3: page-level text detection

New stage. Detects and classifies text regions — lyrics, dynamics, tempo, staff text, expression — to feed a recogniser crops.

Supervision is OCR-first: run OCR over each scanned page, keep only reads that also match the score's own symbolic text. Free real-scan boxes, no manual annotation. The catch is that a box exists only where OCR and the score agree, so text the OCR missed is unlabelled — and calling it background trains the detector to miss exactly what it's for.

We ran the ablation instead of arguing: three masking policies × two loss configs.

Real-scan data helps a lot — the synthetic-only baseline gets 0.581 patch IoU on lyrics on real scans, 0.966 with scan data. It's not bad at lyrics generally (0.955 on synthetic); it's bad at lyrics on scans.

The structural finding: every 2-class run beats the baseline, every all-class run falls below it, across masking policies spanning two orders of magnitude of supervised pixels. One loss can't serve a densely-labelled synthetic class set and a sparsely-labelled scan class set at once. The answer is two models, surfaced in the app as a user-selected vocal/instrumental toggle.

Four things that didn't work

Each of these looked like a good idea. Save yourself the week.

1 · Unfreezing the core to improve conditioning

Score-profile conditioning over a frozen core: mean ablation delta +0.0615, positive in all ten epochs. Identical run, only the freeze policy changed (150 trainable tensors instead of 8): the delta oscillated in sign and ran an order of magnitude below the frozen run's smallest value. Unfreezing didn't improve it — it erased it.

2 · Time-signature conditioning + duration loss

Mean delta +0.0513, slightly lower than profile-only, ranges overlapping heavily. Both mechanisms were enabled in the same run, so nothing isolates either. Honest read: adding both didn't measurably help or hurt. Not "time-signature conditioning works."

3 · Throwing synthetic renders at a scan-adapted model

42,088 staves of the same music, engraved instead of photographed — the biggest untapped source we had. Scan accuracy got worse at every weight (0.96906 → 0.96863 at full, 0.96847 at 0.4). Lower weight = less damage, never a sign flip. 15% general replay didn't prevent it. Useful consequence: the model isn't data-limited on staves of this kind, so more of the same won't help you either.

4 · The masking policy that should have won

"Blank-paper" masking labels unmatched white pixels background and leaves unmatched inked pixels ignored — blank paper is certainly not text, so those negatives are free. Lifts supervision 1.98% → 86.2%. We predicted it'd keep E1's recall and gain E2's precision. It gained nothing (78.8% vs 78.5% precision). So E2 wins by labelling ambiguous inked regions background — the one thing this policy refuses to do. Obvious in hindsight: detectors don't fire on blank paper.

And then we did it anyway

We trained the instrumental detector with the blank-paper policy after writing that conclusion down. It emitted 4,378 boxes per page against ~15 real ones. Patch IoU hid it completely (0.853 on Tempo while emitting 92,735 tempo boxes); background IoU sitting at 0.360 was the warning we didn't act on. A conclusion recorded is not a conclusion applied.

Retrained on background-outside masks: Dynamic precision 0.1% → 43.1%, recall 32.5% → 75.7%, overall recall 71%. Rarer classes still over-predict (9.7–22.2% precision). Note this ground truth is OCR-confirmed so precision is understated, and the vocal detector's 88.5% used complete engraver annotations — don't compare the two directly.

The expensive one: (page, system) joins between a synthetic-paginated symbolic directory and scanned crops. Different editions paginate differently — 24 pages vs 22 — and because both dirs held the same segment count, the join found a crop, part counts matched, every guard passed. 56.7% of scanned staves were labelled with the wrong music. Now 7.9%.

Two more came from a review page that renders labels back into notation beside the scan, and from nothing else: 2.4% of staves had no clef token (invisible — pitches are absolute, so it costs nothing in accuracy and shows up only when something reconstructs the staff), and every barline was stripped upstream — 8,617 in the whole scores, 3,740 repeats, zero in training data.

Checks worth adding to your own pipeline

Do your scanned and synthetic tracks paginate identically? Any (page, system) join is wrong if not. · Count clef tokens — a corpus with 2% missing trains and evaluates normally. · Count barlines in segments vs. whole scores; zero is a real possibility. · Render your labels and look at them.

If you want to build on this

Turning the heads on is a config change plus trained weights:

config.enable_structured_heads = True
# head names come from the manifest, not from assuming the full set —
# a name absent from it is UNSUPPORTED, not "predicts nothing"
manifest = load("heads_manifest.json")

Training them yourself needs token files with notation sidecars (beam/stem/slur labels per note) and a frozen core — train_structured_heads.py enforces the freeze and refuses to run without heads to train.

Two corpora of OCR-first text ground truth are prepared for release, derived from openly-licensed OpenScore editions:
