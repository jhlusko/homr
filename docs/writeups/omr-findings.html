Technical report · Optical Music Recognition

Extending homr for Ensemble Scores

Structured notation heads, page-level text detection, and scan-domain adaptation, measured against the upstream baseline

Working report · all figures from held-out splits

Abstract

homr is a transformer-based OMR system that reads one staff crop and emits a token sequence over rhythm, pitch, lift, articulation, slur and position. It cannot represent beaming, stem direction, ties or slur spans, has no page-level text stage, and is trained predominantly on synthetic renders.

We report three classes of change and their measured effect. (i) Staff-level recognition: against the upstream checkpoint on identical held-out staves, every output branch improves — by 2.1–3.5 points on vocal scans and 1.8–6.3 points on instrumental scans. (ii) Representational capability: eleven structured heads trained over a frozen core recover the complete six-level beam state for 95.1% of notes, plus slur spans at 0.929 and ties at 0.803 — none of which the baseline can express at all. (iii) Decode-time structure: cross-staff consistency reranking reduces structural findings across 200 pages by 20.8% with zero pages made worse.

We also report what did not work: unfreezing the core erases a conditioning gain rather than improving it; adding 42,088 synthetic staves reduces scan accuracy at every weight tried; and a masking policy predicted to dominate its alternatives gained nothing. The corpus corrections that made the staff-level result possible — a pagination join that mislabelled 56.7% of scanned staves, 2.4% of staves missing clefs, and every barline stripped upstream — are described because they were invisible to every metric we were watching.

1Baseline and scope

What homr does, and the constraint we imposed

homr's recognition core is an encoder–decoder over a single staff crop. Its token vocabulary factors into six branches, which the decoder predicts jointly. That vocabulary has no representation for beam connectivity, stem direction, tie state, slur extent, or dynamics as attached objects; nor does the system detect text regions on a page.

Our target is multi-staff ensemble scores. The governing constraint was not to produce a corpus-specific model: OpenScore String Quartets is the first adaptation corpus, not the definition of the system, and every new interface is optional, variable-staff and instrument-agnostic.

All comparisons below use the upstream pinned checkpoint as baseline, evaluated through the same loader and metric as our checkpoint. Where a capability does not exist upstream we say so rather than reporting a delta against zero.

2Architectural discipline: additive and zero-gated

The property that makes the rest contributable

Every addition ships disabled by default, under a standing rule: a checkpoint trained without a feature must keep loading and behaving exactly as before. Structured heads, score-profile conditioning, a duration-adherence loss term and a cross-staff context adapter are all gated off; several have zero-initialised gates, so enabling them is still a no-op until training moves the gate.

Checkpoint loading validates an allowlist of expected new parameters and rejects everything else, so an absent core weight is an error rather than a silent reinitialisation. New semantics become new heads, never an expanded rhythm vocabulary — expanding it would alter the largest existing softmax and embedding matrices and entangle notation fidelity with note-sequence accuracy.

A second commitment shaped the results more than expected: factor independent musical dimensions rather than emitting combinatorial tokens such as slur2StartAbove_beam2BackwardHook_stemDown. Beam level, beam state, stem direction, slur identity, slur event and slur side have distinct semantics and wildly distinct frequencies. Giving each its own head and loss makes rarity visible: a head with eight supported examples in the validation split announces itself, where a combinatorial token would bury the same sparsity inside an aggregate.

Both checkpoints were scored on identical held-out splits using free-running generation with a length-mismatch penalty: predictions and references are padded to the longer length with a sentinel that cannot match, so a length disagreement counts against the model rather than being scored only on the overlap.

On reading Table 1

Absolute levels differ sharply between domains because the tasks differ: OSSQ staves are single-part quartet lines, Lieder staves are dense piano-and-voice writing. Compare columns within a domain, not across. The metric is stricter than teacher-forced token accuracy — both models are scored identically, so the deltas hold, but these numbers are not comparable to eval_accuracy figures elsewhere.

One limitation must be stated plainly: this is a combined delta. Corrected pagination, carried clefs, restored barlines and scan-domain adaptation all landed in the same checkpoint. Table 1 demonstrates that the programme worked; it does not attribute the gain to any individual change, and we did not run the ablation that would.

4Structured notation heads

Capability the baseline does not have

Eleven heads predict, per note: six beam levels, stem direction, tie state, dynamic mark, and per-slot slur event and side. They read the decoder hidden state and are trained with the core entirely frozen — 326 core parameters loaded and locked, 22 head tensors trainable.

Freezing is a measurement decision, not an optimisation. If the core moved as well, a gain could be the heads or the core drifting to suit them, and the result would not be attributable.

4.1Two rows that are not results

Beam level 4 has eight supported positions in the entire held-out split; 0.5972 is arithmetic. The design asked whether levels 5–6 have enough examples for learned prediction — level 4 already answers it, and they should be deterministic or unsupported.

Dynamics sits at 0.103 with the largest support of any head. Our first explanation, that recovery was failing to deliver marks, was wrong: 94.5% of notes carry no dynamic (musically correct) and the labelled remainder spreads across eleven classes, five holding fewer than thirteen examples. Macro-F1 weights classes equally, so a head that learns p, f and sf and cannot learn fz scores near 0.10 regardless. A rarity problem, not a delivery problem; the remedy is reporting or class merging.

4.2A trained head is not a feature

The heads predicted beams at 95% for some time before we noticed that homr's MusicXML generator contained zero beam references. Every prediction was discarded on output, and renders showed the engraver's automatic beaming — which a round-trip measurement had already shown rewrites the grouping of 1.7% of notes, its largest single pattern converting backward hooks to full beams.

Nine passing unit tests were not evidence the feature worked. The end-to-end check on a real staff — 20 beam elements, begin 5 / continue 10 / end 5, five coherent groups — was.

5Decode-time cross-staff reranking

The largest end-to-end quality result

Ensemble scores provide a constraint a single-staff decoder cannot use: sibling staves in the same system must agree on where barlines fall. We exploit this at decode time rather than by retraining. At each staff's narrowest-margin rhythm decisions we fork a full alternate decode, then keep whichever candidate — greedy or fork — best matches the other staves' majority cumulative barline positions.

This was built deliberately narrower than fixed-width beam search, which would require per-step KV-cache batching the codebase has never used.

A 20-page pilot showed a 48% reduction; the full 200-page run gives 20.8%. We report the larger sample. Zero regressions across 200 pages is the property that matters for a decode-time intervention — it can be enabled without a quality risk elsewhere.

This sits on top of deterministic cross-staff analysis: eight consistency checks run in the live pipeline, firing on 71.4% of pages, with targeted repair proposals on 14.6%. The design's ordering — deterministic checks, then targeted repair, then a learned adapter only if needed — held; the learned adapter remains unbuilt and zero-gated.

6Corpus corrections

Necessary for §3, and invisible to every metric we watched

The scanned track's symbolic content was drawn from a directory keyed to the synthetic pagination and joined to scanned crops by (page, system). The two editions paginate differently — one quartet renders to 24 pages and scans to 22 — and because both directories hold the same number of segments, the join found a crop, the part count matched, and every guard passed.

The decisive evidence was a single comparison: on one collapsed staff, the model's reading of the scan matched the correct symbolic source exactly and the training label not at all. The model was reading correctly and being scored against the wrong answer.

Two further defects came from a review surface that renders labels back into notation beside their scans, and from no metric:

  • 2.4% of staves carried no clef token. Invisible because pitches in this format are absolute — a missing clef costs nothing in pitch accuracy and moves no number in any log. It appears only when something reconstructs the staff.
  • Every barline had been stripped by the round trip producing the segments: 8,617 in the whole scores, 3,740 of them repeats, zero in the training data. Reviewers reported it as "correct but missing final repeat".

All three are repaired by rebuilding from the whole score by positional join, gated on an alignment verified independently. Fifty-two human verdicts on the worst-scoring staves after the fix returned zero mismatched pairs.

7Page-level text detection

A stage the baseline does not have

A new stage detects and classifies text regions — lyrics, dynamics, tempo, staff text, expression — to feed a recogniser. Training supervision is generated OCR-first: run OCR over each scanned page, keep only reads that match the score's own symbolic text. This yields real-scan boxes with no manual annotation, and an asymmetry: a box exists only where OCR and the score agree, so text the OCR missed is unlabelled.

Real-scan data helps substantially: on real scans the synthetic-only baseline reaches 0.581 patch IoU on lyrics against 0.966 with scan data. The baseline is not weak at lyrics generally — it scores 0.955 on synthetic pages — it is weak on real scans specifically.

The structural result is that every 2-class run beats the baseline and every all-class run falls below it, across masking policies spanning two orders of magnitude of supervised pixels. Adding sparse two-class real-scan supervision to a loss also serving six densely-labelled synthetic classes damages the two classes the new data is about. The conclusion is separate models rather than a better mask, surfaced in the application as a user-selected vocal/instrumental toggle.

8Negative results

Reported because each was expected to work

8.1Unfreezing erases a conditioning gain

Optional score-profile conditioning (instrument family, clef, staff count, transposition) trained over a frozen core gives a mean ablation delta of +0.0615, positive in all ten epochs. An identical run differing only in freeze policy — 150 trainable tensors instead of 8 — did not improve on it: the delta oscillated in sign and ran an order of magnitude below the frozen run's smallest value. Unfreezing did not improve the result; it erased the measurable effect.

8.2Time-signature conditioning: a null result

A later run added time-signature conditioning and a duration-adherence loss. Mean delta +0.0513 — slightly lower than the profile-only run, with heavily overlapping per-epoch ranges. Both mechanisms were enabled together, so nothing isolates either. The fair statement is that adding both did not measurably help or hurt the existing signal.

8.3Synthetic renders reduce scan accuracy

The corpus's own synthetic track — 42,088 staves of the same music engraved rather than photographed — was the largest untapped data source available. Added to a scan-adapted model it made scan accuracy worse at every weight tried (0.96906 baseline → 0.96863 at full weight, 0.96847 at 0.4). Lowering the weight reduces the damage without flipping the sign; a 15% general-data replay did not prevent it. The useful consequence is a bound: the model is not data-limited on staves of this kind.

8.4A masking policy that should have won

The "blank-paper" policy labels unmatched white pixels background and leaves unmatched inked pixels ignored, on the reasoning that blank paper is certainly not text. It lifts supervision from 1.98% to 86.2%. We predicted it would retain E1's recall and gain E2's precision; it landed on E1's precision almost exactly (78.8% vs 78.5%). E2's advantage therefore does not come from more negative supervision but from labelling ambiguous inked regions background — the one thing this policy refuses to do.

We then trained the instrumental detector with that policy anyway, and it emitted 4,378 boxes per page against roughly fifteen real ones. The failure and its cause were already documented; the documentation did not reach the decision.

Retrained on background-outside masks, the failure largely resolves — Dynamic precision moves from 0.1% to 43.1% and recall from 32.5% to 75.7% (F1 54.9%); overall recall doubles to 71.0%. Precision on the three rarer classes remains poor (9.7–22.2%). Two caveats govern that number: this ground truth is OCR-confirmed, so a correct detection of text the OCR missed scores as a false positive, and the vocal detector's 88.5% was measured against complete engraver box annotations. The two detectors' figures are therefore not directly comparable, and the instrumental model is not yet at a shippable precision.

9Threats to validity

What these numbers do not establish

  • Table 1 is a combined delta. Four changes landed in one checkpoint; no ablation isolates them.
  • Patch-level and page-level metrics disagree in this domain. A configuration leading the patch table (0.966 real-scan lyrics) came last of six on page-level F1. We report page-level wherever it exists.
  • Macro metrics over rare classes report noise. Three separate instances: a class at 0.875 that was 7/8 on a handful of patches, a beam level scoring on eight positions, a dynamics head dominated by five classes with fewer than thirteen examples.
  • OCR-derived detector ground truth understates precision, since a correct detection of text the OCR missed counts as a false positive. Recall is sound.
  • Single-seed runs. No variance estimates; small deltas should be treated accordingly.
